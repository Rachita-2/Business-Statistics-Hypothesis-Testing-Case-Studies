# Business Statistics & Hypothesis Testing — Five Applied Case Studies

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates the application of descriptive statistics and hypothesis testing to five business scenarios using Python.

The objective is to convert business questions into appropriate statistical analyses and translate statistical results into practical business insights.

## Business Problems

### 1. Lending Club Loan Analysis
Analysis of the relationship between:
- Loan amount and interest rate
- Loan tenure and interest rate
- Loan purpose and interest rate
- FICO score and home ownership

### 2. Pricing Expert Consistency
Comparison of price quotations provided by two pricing experts for the same orders.

### 3. Reengineering Effectiveness
Evaluation of whether a reengineering initiative affected:
- Employee turnover
- Temporary removals
- Critical incidents

### 4. Job Priority System Effectiveness
Assessment of whether high, medium and low priority jobs are being completed according to the intended prioritization system.

### 5. Film on the Rocks Customer Analysis
Analysis of:
- Overall customer satisfaction
- Factors associated with satisfaction
- Customer demographics
- Marketing and awareness channels

## Statistical Techniques Used

- Descriptive Statistics
- Pearson Correlation
- Welch's t-test
- Paired t-test
- One-Way ANOVA
- Chi-Square Test of Independence
- Spearman Rank Correlation

## Tools & Libraries

- Python
- Pandas
- NumPy
- SciPy
- Matplotlib
- Jupyter Notebook

## Key Business Insights

- Larger requested loan amounts were associated with higher interest rates in the lending dataset.
- Longer-term loans carried significantly higher average interest rates.
- Average price quotations differed significantly between the two pricing experts.
- The reengineering initiative improved resident-related behavioral measures but did not significantly improve employee turnover.
- The job-priority system did not demonstrate the intended completion-time ordering.
- Film on the Rocks showed high overall customer satisfaction, with parking having the strongest measured association with overall satisfaction.
- Word of mouth was the dominant customer awareness source.
